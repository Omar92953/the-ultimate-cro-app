To raise average order value on Shopify, add one offer at a time at the moments shoppers decide: the product page first, then the cart, then right after payment. Measure before you start and after each step, so you know which change did the work. The plan below fits into 30 days: one week to measure, then one new offer each week, and a review at the end.

## What is average order value, and why follow a plan?

Average order value (AOV) is your sales divided by your number of orders over the same period. For example, $4,000 of sales from 100 orders is an AOV of $40. Shopify shows it in **Analytics**.

A plan helps because changing five things on the same day tells you nothing. If AOV goes up, you don't know which change caused it, and if it goes down, you don't know what to undo. One change a week gives each offer a few days of real orders before the next one starts. For more ideas beyond this plan, see [how to increase average order value on Shopify](/increase-aov-shopify/).

## Week 1: measure your AOV and pick 3 products

Don't add any offer this week. Write down where you start.

1. In Shopify's **Analytics**, note your average order value and your number of orders for the last 30 days. Note your conversion rate too, so you can check later that bigger orders didn't cost you orders.
2. Look at your recent orders and find your typical order total: the amount most orders sit around. You will use it in week 3.
3. Pick 3 products:
   - **A best seller** for the product page offer in week 2.
   - **A small item that goes with many things**, such as a refill, a case or a care product, for the cart in week 3.
   - **A useful extra** for the offer after payment in week 4.
4. Install CRO Toolbox and turn on the **Conversion boosters** app embed. The quickest way is **Settings → Theme and app embeds** in the app; the steps are in [turn on the app embeds](/help/turn-on-app-embeds/).

If your store gets only a few orders a week, weekly numbers will jump around. In that case run each step for two weeks instead of one, and compare longer periods.

## Week 2: one product page offer

The product page is where the shopper chooses how much to buy. Add one offer there, on your best seller. Pick the type that fits the product:

- **Quantity breaks** for products people use up or buy in pairs: Buy 2, save 10%; Buy 3, save 15%. See [quantity breaks that raise average order value](/blog/quantity-breaks-that-raise-aov/).
- **Frequently bought together** for two or three products that are used together. See [how to add frequently bought together to Shopify](/blog/how-to-add-frequently-bought-together-to-shopify/).
- **A cross sell** for a product that pairs well, ticked and added in one click.

To set up quantity breaks:

1. Open **Offers → Upsell offers** and create an offer.
2. Choose which products get it: all products, specific products or products in collections. Start with your best seller only.
3. Add two or three tiers: quantity, discount and an optional label and badge, such as "Most popular".
4. Open **Style** to pick a look and set the wording, colours and corners with the live preview.
5. Click **Add to theme**, place the block above your Add to cart button and click **Save** in the theme editor.
6. Place a test order and check the price at checkout.

The tier the shopper picks sets the quantity on your normal Add to cart button, and the discount applies at checkout once the cart reaches the tier's quantity. More on [upsells on Shopify](/shopify-upsell-app/).

**What to measure this week:** the share of orders with two or more units of that product, and the sales the offer brings in **Analytics** in the app.

## Week 3: a cart offer and a free shipping goal

The cart is where the shopper looks at the order total. Two things work well together there: a small item that completes the order, and a free shipping goal they can see.

1. Open **Cart & Checkout → Free shipping bar** and set the goal a little above your typical order from week 1. For example, if most orders are around $40, a goal of $50 is a reachable next step.
2. Keep **Give free shipping automatically** on. Saving creates the automatic discount "CRO Toolbox free shipping" for orders from the goal amount.
3. Open **Offers → Cross sell offers** and create an offer: the trigger products, your small item from week 1 as the offered product, and the cart drawer and cart page in where it appears.
4. In **Cart & Checkout**, switch on **Offers in your theme's cart** to show it inside your theme's own cart drawer. This works with Dawn and Dawn based themes; with other themes, use the cart page.
5. Add something to the cart on your store and check the drawer, the bar and the checkout total.

Before you set the goal, check that you can afford free shipping on orders at that amount. The [cart help guide](/help/cart/) has the details, and [cart drawer upsells that work](/blog/cart-drawer-upsells-that-work/) covers what to offer in the drawer.

**What to measure this week:** AOV, how many orders reach the free shipping goal, and how often the cart offer is added.

## Week 4: an offer after payment, then review

The last offer comes after the order is paid. Because the first order is already placed, it can't put that sale at risk.

1. Open **Offers → Upsell → After purchase**.
2. Pick your useful extra from week 1 and the discount. If you want, turn on **Only for bigger orders** and set the amount in **Orders from**.
3. Write the heading and text, and switch the offer on.
4. In Shopify, open **Settings → Checkout** and under **Post purchase page** choose CRO Toolbox.
5. Place a test order and check the offer after payment.

Accepting adds the product to the same order without entering payment details again. Shopify shows after purchase offers for card and Shop Pay payments. If you also want to bring shoppers back, the thank you page can show a discount code for the next order; see the [checkout help guide](/help/checkout/).

At the end of the week, review the whole month against your week 1 numbers, over the same number of days. Keep the offers that raised AOV without lowering your conversion rate. Change or switch off the rest, one at a time.

## What should I measure?

- **Average order value** in Shopify's Analytics, always over the same number of days before and after.
- **Number of orders and conversion rate.** A higher AOV with fewer orders is not a win.
- **Units per order**, or the share of orders with more than one item.
- **Sales from each offer** in CRO Toolbox's **Analytics**: the last 30 days compared with the 30 before, with results for each offer and bundle. The Free plan shows its own results for 1 offer, Starter ($5.99 a month) for 3, and Growth ($14.99 a month) for every offer.
- **The holdout test**, on the Growth plan and above: a share of shoppers sees no app widgets, so you can compare what shoppers with and without them spend. See [analytics and the holdout test](/help/analytics/).
- **What the discounts cost you.** Bigger orders at a lower margin may not leave you better off.

## Which plan does this need?

The Free plan allows 3 offers on at once, which covers the three offers in this plan: one on the product page, one in the cart and one after payment. The free shipping bar is also on the Free plan. Paid plans start with Starter at $5.99 a month, with unlimited offers. See [pricing](/pricing/).

## Quick checklist

- Week 1: AOV, orders and conversion rate written down, typical order found, 3 products picked
- Week 2: one product page offer on your best seller, test order checked
- Week 3: free shipping goal a little above your typical order, one small cart offer
- Week 4: one offer after payment, then a review against week 1
- One change at a time, compared over the same number of days
