To set a minimum order amount on Shopify, you need an app with order limits: Shopify has no setting for a minimum order total on a regular store. An order limit checks the cart at checkout and stops the order, with your own message, when it is below your minimum total, has too few items, or has too many of one product. CRO Toolbox has order limits on every plan, including Free. Below is when a minimum makes sense, how to pick the number, and how to set it up.

## Why would I set a minimum order?

- **Shipping and packing costs.** If a small order costs more to pick, pack and ship than it earns, a minimum keeps every order worth sending.
- **Wholesale.** Trade buyers often order from a minimum amount or a minimum number of units.
- **Cheap products.** Stickers, samples or spare parts sold one at a time can lose money on every order.
- **Local delivery.** A minimum for delivery runs makes each trip worth it.

## Why would I set a maximum?

- **Limited drops and launches.** A limit per product stops one buyer taking the whole stock.
- **Resellers.** A cap on items in one order makes bulk buying for resale harder.
- **Risk.** A maximum order total stops very large orders at checkout, so a big buyer contacts you first instead of ordering in one click.

## Can Shopify set a minimum order without an app?

Not for regular shoppers. Shopify's B2B features can set quantity rules on wholesale catalogs, but a normal store has no minimum order setting. Apps add it through Shopify's checkout validation: once a limit is saved in an app, Shopify checks it at checkout on its own, and the shopper sees your message until the cart meets it.

## How do I pick the minimum amount?

Start from your numbers, not a round figure:

1. Work out what an order costs you to pick, pack and ship.
2. Find the order total where your margin covers that cost.
3. Look at your average order value. A minimum far above it will turn many shoppers away, so set it near or below what most people already spend.
4. Check the prices of items shoppers often add, so a small top up reaches the minimum.

If you only want bigger orders, not to block small ones, a free shipping threshold may be the gentler choice. Our guide to [free shipping threshold maths](/blog/free-shipping-threshold-maths/) walks through the numbers.

## How do I set an order limit with CRO Toolbox?

1. Open **Cart & Checkout → Order limits** in the app.
2. Add a limit and pick its type: **Minimum order total**, **Maximum order total**, **Minimum items in the order**, **Maximum items in the order**, **Minimum quantity per product** or **Maximum quantity per product**.
3. Type the amount or quantity. For a limit per product, choose the products it applies to.
4. Write the message shoppers see at checkout, or leave it empty for the default, such as "The minimum order is $30." Use {limit} for the number and {product} for the product's name.
5. Switch the limit on and save. You can add more limits and move them up or down.
6. Place one test order that breaks the limit and one that meets it, and check what checkout shows.

The same screen has payment rules and delivery rules. Details: [checkout rules help guide](/help/checkout/).

## How do I tell shoppers about the minimum before checkout?

A limit that only appears at checkout can surprise people. Say it earlier:

- A line in your announcement bar, such as "Minimum order $30".
- A note on your shipping or FAQ page.
- A cart message or free shipping bar that shows how much more to add.

## Quick checklist

- Your cost to pick, pack and ship one order
- A minimum near or below your average order value
- Items shoppers can add to reach it
- Per product limits for drops and limited stock
- A clear message at checkout with the amount
- The minimum shown in the announcement bar or on your FAQ page
- Two test orders: one below the limit and one above
